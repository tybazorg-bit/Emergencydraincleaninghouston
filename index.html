Why Houston Sewer Pipes Clog Frequently

Most common drain blockages in the Houston metro fall into three specific categories:

  • Expansive Clay Soil Shifting: Houston's gumbo clay soil swells during rainy seasons and contracts during hot summers. This ground movement stresses underground sewer pipes, causing joint misalignments, belly sagging, and fractures.
  • Aggressive Tree Root Infiltration: Trees and shrubbery naturally search for underground moisture, infiltrating tiny fissures in clay or cast iron pipe joints. Left unchecked, root masses trap grease and toilet paper, causing full line blockages.
  • Grease and Mineral Scale Accumulation: Hard water minerals combine with kitchen grease to create hardened scale along the pipe interior. When standard plunging fails, high-pressure hydro jetting is the proven standard for scouring inner pipe walls clean.

Key Indicators of a Main Line Blockage

  • Multiple fixtures backing up simultaneously (e.g. running the washing machine causes toilets or shower drains to gurgle).
  • Persistent sewage odors near exterior cleanouts or bathroom traps.
  • Water rising in shower pans when an adjacent toilet is flushed.
